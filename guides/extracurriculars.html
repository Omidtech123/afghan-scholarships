How to build extracurricular activities

Strong activities are sustained, genuine, and useful—not expensive or famous.

Start with a real need

Look around you: tutoring younger students, a reading group, a women-in-tech club, a local research project, an art initiative, or translating public information can all begin small.

Choose depth over quantity

One activity developed for six months with clear results is more convincing than many one-day certificates. Set a goal, regular tasks, and a simple measure of progress.

Develop leadership naturally

Leadership can mean listening, organizing, teaching, solving a problem, or helping a group continue. You do not need an official title; explain the challenge, action, and result.

Build useful skills

Learn skills through the activity: planning, public speaking, research, design, coding, budgeting, or teamwork. Keep a record of how you applied them.

Document your work

Keep dates, outcomes, safe photos or links, certificates, and contact details for someone who can verify your role. Protect people’s privacy and do not exaggerate impact.

Next step

Choose one activity you can start this month and write a simple 90-day plan.
