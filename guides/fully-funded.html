How to find fully funded scholarships

A repeatable search system is more useful than applying randomly.

Step 1: define your filters

List your degree, field, expected graduation date, grades, English level, nationality or residence status, and countries you can realistically consider. Add any non-negotiables such as full living support or an English-taught course.

Step 2: search official sources

Start with government scholarship portals, university funding pages, recognized international organizations, and embassy education pages. Rah helps you discover programmes; always open the official page and read the current call.

Step 3: understand “fully funded”

Check separately for tuition, monthly stipend, accommodation, insurance, travel, visa, and book costs. “Full scholarship” may not cover every arrival cost. Write what remains your responsibility.

Step 4: assess fit before applying

Compare your education, age, field, language, and documents against the rules. If one requirement is unclear, email the official programme address with a short question. Do not spend weeks on an application you cannot submit.

Step 5: use a deadline tracker

Record the official URL, deadline and time zone, referee deadline, documents, and next action. Begin with programmes closing in three to six months because translations, references, and test dates take time.

Common mistakes

Do not trust old calls, pay agents for a “guaranteed” scholarship, or assume university admission includes funding. Save copies of every submitted form and confirmation email.

Action checklist

Choose three realistic programmes this week, verify them on official sites, and add their deadlines and documents to your tracker.
