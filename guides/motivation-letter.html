How to write a motivation letter

Your letter should connect your evidence, your programme, and your future contribution.

Step 1: decode the prompt

Highlight every question and note the word limit. Most prompts ask some version of: Why you? Why this course or scholarship? What will you do afterward? Answer each part directly.

Step 2: build an evidence list

Write five real examples from education, work, responsibilities, volunteering, or projects. For each one, note the challenge, your action, and result. Use the strongest two or three.

Step 3: use a four-part outline

Open with your goal. Then give evidence of preparation. Explain exactly why this programme fits your next step. Finish with a realistic plan for applying the learning in your profession or community.

Make it specific

Replace “I am a leader” with a short example of organizing, teaching, or solving a problem. Replace “I want to help Afghanistan” with a field, group, or issue you hope to work on.

Tailor every version

Read the scholarship’s mission and the course modules. Mention only details you can genuinely connect to your plan. A generic letter sent to many scholarships is easy to recognize.

Editing checklist

Use your own voice, remove unsupported claims, follow the limit, and ask a trusted reader to check clarity. Keep a master version, but revise it for every application.
