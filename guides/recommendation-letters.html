How to get strong recommendation letters

Good recommendations are planned early and written by people who know your work.

Choose the right person

Pick a teacher, supervisor, mentor, or project leader who can give examples of your work, growth, reliability, and character. Follow the scholarship’s rule about academic or professional referees.

Ask early

Ask at least three weeks before the deadline. Explain the scholarship, due date, and submission method. Give the person a comfortable way to decline if they cannot write a strong letter.

Prepare a referee package

Send your CV, transcript, scholarship description, draft motivation letter, and a short list of experiences they have personally seen. This gives context without asking them to copy your words.

Make submission easy

Tell them whether the programme sends an email link, needs a signed PDF, or uses a portal. Check their email address carefully and let them know when the request is sent.

Follow up respectfully

Send one short reminder about a week before the deadline. Thank them afterward and share the result when available.

Avoid these mistakes

Do not ask the most senior person if they do not know you, wait until the last week, or submit a letter in someone else’s name without their approval.
