How to create a scholarship CV

Your CV should make your preparation and potential easy to understand quickly.

Choose the right length

One page is ideal for many students; two pages is enough when you have substantial research or work experience. Use a simple readable layout and export a PDF.

Use a clear order

Include contact details, education, experience, projects or volunteering, skills and languages, awards, and publications if relevant. Put your strongest relevant section near the top.

Write achievement bullets

Use action + result: “Organized a weekly study group for 15 students” is stronger than “Leadership.” Include dates, your role, scale, and outcome.

Show informal experience too

Family responsibilities, self-study, community work, online projects, and mentoring can all be relevant when presented honestly with clear responsibilities and outcomes.

Tailor it to the opportunity

For a technical scholarship, lead with projects and science work. For a leadership programme, make service and initiative visible. Never add skills or achievements you cannot explain in an interview.

Final checklist

Use consistent dates, a professional email, no spelling errors, and a file name such as Firstname_Lastname_CV.pdf.
